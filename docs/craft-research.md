## THE TWELVE - Core Craft Rules

### 1. BOLD-LINE SKIMMABILITY: Structure for 90% skimmers
**Source:** AcelleMail (acellemail.com) + research on email skim times  
**Rule:** 90% of recipients skim emails in 5-10 seconds. Bold key sentences at the start of each paragraph and use single-sentence paragraphs for critical info. Readers who skim should grasp the entire meaning from bold text alone.  
**Before/After:**  
- BEFORE: "We are pleased to announce that our new feature rollout has been completed and is now available to all users."
- AFTER: "**Your new feature is live.** We finished rollout yesterday and all users can access it now.

### 2. FRONT-LOAD SUBJECT LINES: 40 characters, most important words first
**Source:** Mailpro (The Best Email Subject Line Length in 2026); research on mobile truncation  
**Rule:** Subject lines show 33-43 characters on mobile. Put your core news in the first 7 words (40 chars max). Every character after that is likely cut off.  
**Example:** "ZABAL Gamez Finals: 6 artists battle live" (40 chars) beats "The ZABAL Gamez Season 1 championship finals are happening this week with six finalist artists competing in head-to-head battles" (truncated to "The ZABAL Gamez Season 1 championship...").

### 3. VISUALIZABLE LANGUAGE: Specific nouns over adjectives
**Source:** Harry Dry's Marketing Examples (sproutworth.com); Concrete vs Abstract Language research  
**Rule:** Write concrete, specific details readers can picture. Never use vague adjectives ("great," "amazing"); replace them with specific nouns and verbs.  
**Before/After:**  
- BEFORE: "Join us for an exciting finals event with amazing live music battles."
- AFTER: "Six artists battle on WaveWarZ's live-traded music platform Thursday-Friday. Champions split 300 USDC."

### 4. FALSIFIABLE CLAIMS: Numbers that can be checked
**Source:** Harry Dry's three rules (sproutworth.com)  
**Rule:** Make claims specific enough to verify or disprove. "15 entrants narrowed to 6 finalists" beats "Many artists competed" because it can be checked.  
**Benefit:** Readers trust falsifiable claims more; they prove you've done the math.

### 5. OPEN LOOPS: Tease before you reveal
**Source:** The Copy Brothers (thecopybrothers.com); Loewenstein curiosity research  
**Rule:** Plant an unresolved question or hint at an unknown that readers will want to satisfy. Close the loop later in the same piece so trust is maintained.  
**Example:** "One artist went from 0 to 2,000 trades in one week. Here's how." (reveals at end: which artist, what they did).

### 6. PERSONAL LETTER TONE: Write to one, not a list
**Source:** Ann Handley / Campaign Monitor (Ann Handley grew Total Annarchy)  
**Rule:** Address readers as one person ("you," "your") not a segment ("our community"). Handley removed pop-ups and worded newsletters like personal correspondence, which grew her list 42k.  
**Example:** "You're three days from the finals. Here's what to watch for." beats "Subscribers should prepare for the finals event."

### 7. STAKES FIRST, THEN MECHANICS: Why before how
**Source:** Story Stakes (Save the Cat); announcement structure research  
**Rule:** Tell readers WHY this matters—what's at stake, what changes—before explaining HOW to participate or what happens next. Readers need to understand importance to care about details.  
**Structure:** (1) Hook + Stakes (2 sentences), (2) Why This Matters (1-2 sentences), (3) What's Happening (mechanics), (4) Call to Action.

### 8. ONE-THING FOCUS: Announce one central news, not five
**Source:** Apple product announcements; launch email best practices  
**Rule:** Each announcement should have ONE central claim. Apple's AirPods Pro email leads with "Active Noise Cancellation" not five features. If you have five wins, write five separate announces.  
**For ZABAL finals:** Central news is "finalists locked, battles live Thursday-Friday, anyone can watch on WaveWarZ."

### 9. HEADLINES 3-10 WORDS, LIKE DESCRIBING TO A FRIEND
**Source:** The Hustle / Sam Parr (thehustle.co/write-like-hustle); research on headline length  
**Rule:** Headlines should be testable with friends—would you use these exact words telling a friend? "Six Artists Clash on WaveWarZ Live Friday" works; "The Intersection of Decentralized Music Platforms and Competitive Artist Ecosystems" does not.

### 10. SHORT SENTENCES, SHORTER PARAGRAPHS: Max 25 words/sentence, 2-4 per paragraph
**Source:** The Hustle (Sam Parr); David Perell's guidelines; Hemingway Editor targets  
**Rule:** Aim for 7th-8th grade reading level. Readers lose comprehension in sentences over 25 words. Never pile more than 4 sentences in one paragraph.  
**Before/After:**  
- BEFORE: "Following a three-month period of intensive workshops and submissions involving a diverse cohort of participants, we have narrowed the field to six finalists who will now proceed to the championship stage."
- AFTER: "Three months of workshops. 31 projects submitted. 6 finalists locked. Now for the live battles."

### 11. WHITE SPACE IS OXYGEN: Visual breathing room
**Source:** Ann Handley / Total Annarchy growth  
**Rule:** Handley grew her list partly by using white space—one-sentence lines, generous line breaks, visually skimmable blocks. Dense text makes readers exit.  
**Tactic:** Never write a paragraph over 3 lines on a newsletter. Use bullet points, line breaks, and single-sentence lines as "visual breaks."

### 12. NAME THE PEOPLE: Make them heroes, roll-call the creators
**Source:** Community newsletter writing research; member spotlighting tactics  
**Rule:** Name the finalist artists by name (not "our six competitors"). Make THEM the story, not the platform. Roll-call creates belonging and makes subscribers feel seen.  
**Example:** "dee-13 and n3m (artists) face jdwalka and ghostmintops (builders) in the semifinals." Names > roles.

## ANNOUNCEMENT SKELETON

This is the section-by-section shape for a finals-announcement-type post:

### Section 1: HOOK + STAKES (2-3 sentences, max 8 words per sentence)
**Purpose:** Grab in first line. Make stakes clear.  
**Source Justification:** Front-load rule (Mailpro), Open loops rule (Copy Brothers), Stakes-first rule  
**Structure:** Single bold sentence stating the central news, then 1-2 more setting the urgency or stakes.

### Section 2: WHY THIS MATTERS (1-2 sentences)
**Purpose:** Explain impact before mechanics.  
**Source Justification:** Stakes-first rule; Story Stakes research  
**Content:** What does this mean for the community / readers / artists? What's at stake? Why does this moment matter?

### Section 3: THE PEOPLE (roll-call, one line per finalist)
**Purpose:** Make finalists heroes; naming creates belonging.  
**Source Justification:** Community newsletter research; Ann Handley's approach to showcasing members  
**Structure:** Intro line ("Six artists made it. Here they are:") then 3 lines of names, roles, or brief context per track.

### Section 4: WHAT'S HAPPENING (the mechanics, in 4 short bullets or lines)
**Purpose:** Clear, scannable timeline and details.  
**Source Justification:** Bold-line skimmability rule; Hustle structure (5-10 words per line)  
**Content:** Date/time, format, where to watch, prizes structure (in concrete numbers, falsifiable).

### Section 5: HOW TO WATCH / CALL TO ACTION (1-2 sentences + link)
**Purpose:** Frictionless next step.  
**Source Justification:** Announcement structure research  
**Content:** One action verb ("Watch live," "Set a reminder," "Follow on WaveWarZ"). Link to where.

### Section 6: SIGN-OFF (one line)
**Purpose:** Voice and closure.  
**Source Justification:** Personal letter tone (Handley)  
**Content:** Founder voice; author attribution. Not "The ZABAL Team" but "- BetterCallZaal on behalf of..."

## WORKED EXAMPLE

**Newsletter Body:**

---

**zm,**

Three months of free workshops. 31 projects built. 15 artists entered. Tonight we lock the six.

ZABAL Gamez Season 1 finals start Thursday night. Six artists will battle live on WaveWarZ, three will win, and anyone can watch it happen.

This is what you helped build. Here are the six taking it to the finish line.

**THE SIX**

*Artists:* dee-13 and n3m — your two finalists who've been battling all season on WaveWarZ.

*Builders:* jdwalka and ghostmintops — the two who coded their way through the workshop series and made it to the finals.

*Creators:* presdency.eth and uniquebeing404 — your two creator-track finalists.

**WHAT'S HAPPENING**

**Thursday-Friday (last week of August):** Head-to-head battles. Each finalist vs. finalist per track.  
**Live:** Watch on WaveWarZ. No sign-up needed.  
**Judging:** Three track mentors + one more factor (locked soon).  
**Prizes:** 300 USDC per track split 70-30 (1st/2nd). Plus 200 USDC volume-weighted capped at 80 across all traders.

Exact dates locked with the six this week. Watch #zabal-games for the call.

**Watch it live.** Follow @WaveWarZ when it goes. Dates at zabalgamez.com/recordings.

\- BetterCallZaal on behalf of the ZABAL Team

### Analysis of Craft Application

| Craft Rule | How Applied |
|---|---|
| **Bold-line skimmability** | "Three months... 31 projects... 15 artists... Tonight we lock the six." Entire meaning survives 5-second skim from bold opening. |
| **Visualizable language** | Specific numbers (31, 15, 6, 300 USDC, 70-30 split). Artist names, not roles alone. "Live on WaveWarZ" not "competition." |
| **Stakes first** | Opens with "This is what you helped build" before listing mechanics. Readers understand impact before details. |
| **Personal letter** | "zm" (direct), "you" (second person), "Here are the six taking it to the finish line" (speaks to reader's connection). |
| **Short sentences** | Average 8-12 words. Max one paragraph = 3 lines. "Tonight we lock the six" is one sentence. |
| **Open loops** | "Judging: one more factor (locked soon)" teases an unknown closing later in the week. Creates follow-up engagement. |
| **Name the people** | Every finalist named by art handle. "dee-13 and n3m" not "two artists." Roll-call structure. |
| **One-thing focus** | Central news: "Finals start Thursday. Six battle live. Anyone watches." Other info (prizes, dates, judging) supports this. |
| **White space** | Single-sentence section headers. Bullets for scannable info. No paragraph over 3 lines. |
| **Concrete nouns** | "WaveWarZ" (specific platform, not "music app"), "300 USDC" (specific number, not "prizes"), "70-30 split" (falsifiable). |
