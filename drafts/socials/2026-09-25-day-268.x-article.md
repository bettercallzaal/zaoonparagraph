## Draft, to post as an X Article

ZM.

Eight days out from ZAOstock, here is the part of the bill I have said the least about. Five of the eight acts playing the Franklin Street Parklet on October 3 are Maine artists, and three of them live within an hour of the stage.

The Crown Vics open at noon, and they are local, out of the Ellsworth and Bangor area. They call themselves Maine's premier rock 'n' roll dance band, built on rockabilly, boogie woogie and honky tonk, run through classic rock, Americana and indie, and they have played it everywhere from Portland to Halifax, in ski resorts and theaters and corporate rooms and bars. The line in their own bio that decided the slot for me is how they describe a set: no long solos, no slow fades, hardly a breath between numbers. That is what noon needs.

OPEN X play second. Their own site introduces them as fresh from the emerging music scene of Maine, and their recent dates run Ellsworth, Bar Harbor, Lucerne. They are a three-piece that sounds like more than three people: alternative rock blended with progressive rock, heavy metal and modern pop, thick guitar riffs against delicate vocal melodies, Ryan Miller's delivery at the center, basslines and percussion holding up a wall of sound they describe as cinematic and built for a stadium. They are getting a parklet, and I think it will hold.

Grass Rug play third. A Portland rock band formed in 2025, indie jam rock, improvisational live: Sam Mitchell on vocals and rhythm guitar, Jack Howianec on lead, Jacob Mitchell on rhythm, Donny Bowman on bass, Peter Coleman on drums. Their influences run psychedelic 60s and 70s through 90s alternative and 2010s indie, and they play southern, central and downeast Maine, which means a good share of the room will already know them.

Acadia Rising play fourth, and they are from Ellsworth, where the festival is. Original flute and hand percussion, expressive melodies over dynamic rhythms, pieces drawn from landscapes and traditions from around the world and played in a contemporary voice. World rhythms and global fusion, in the middle of the afternoon, in the town they live in.

Michael Anderson plays fifth. Bar Harbor, roots in blues, soul and jazz, a voice paired with keyboard, and a solo set that comes out fuller than one person should manage. Jazz standards through popular hits. His own line about why he does it: "I find it very important to connect with my audience."

Then the ZAO musicians close the day, and each of them already has an edition of their own. DCoop comes in from the DMV and plays 6th. LyonsDen comes in from the Onondaga Reservation and plays 7th. Tom Fellenz comes in from the SF Bay Area and closes it out.

Five local acts and three who travel, one stage, no ticket price. That mix is the whole point. A festival in a small Maine city that only imports its music is a visiting show, and one that only books within the county is a local show. This is both, on the same stage, in one afternoon.

ZAOstock is free, all ages, Saturday October 3, 2026, 12 to 6 pm Eastern on the Franklin Street Parklet in downtown Ellsworth, Maine. It runs as part of the 9th Annual Art of Ellsworth during Maine Craft Weekend. At 6 the street clears and Black Moon Public House next door hosts its own evening.
