# Three Nights in New York

I landed Thursday night with the conference already over. NFT.NYC ran September 1 to 3 and I got in as it was ending, which meant the whole weekend was the tail: the side rooms, the galleries, the after-hours. For meeting people that is the better half anyway.

I had no plan and no schedule past a couple of links people had sent me.

## Thursday

I started at the Canal Street Show at LUME Studios. Volume 6, themed Beyond the Veil, with 70+ artists showing physical and digital work across one building.

The centerpiece was a live art battle: Kat Buglione and Leeaux against Judasaca and Alyssa Stevens. Judasaca and Alyssa took it. Jeffy Waves, City the King and Kae Lash played.

I caught Attabotty at the end of the night, and met Will and fdot there. I also ran into Desultor, who I had not seen in a while and who had shown work earlier in the week.

Five minutes down the road at Bowery Palace, Christiano Covino was running Underground Sound with Yeti MOB and B Nice. Doors at 11, free, going until 4 in the morning. That room turned out to be full of people I knew from onchain music: Jay Connects, Nessy, Mojo the Ghost, Dr Bruce, Mr McFly, Stu and Beamer.

Two events, one block, and I met more people in a night than I would have in a month of posting.

## Friday, and the thing I actually learned

I swung through the tail end of ART NYC, then went up to the Solana offices at the Skyline Tower on my own.

I want to be honest about this part, because it is the useful bit. It was a great space and I got nothing out of it at first. Everyone was working. Heads down, laptops open, doing their jobs. A coworking floor is not a networking room, and walking into one alone is walking into a library.

Then Judasaca came in.

Juan Salazar works as Judasaca, a Colombian artist who bridges traditional work with digital and AR, and the one who had taken the battle the night before. We had been trying to link up for a while and there he was, and the entire day turned over in about ten seconds.

We ended up doing an interview on the roof of the tower, which is the best backdrop I have had for one of these. That conversation would not exist if he had not walked in.

From there we went to meet a friend of his and of Yuzapata, who I missed on this trip, and I got to hear a band play live in the studio. No stage, no lineup, just a band playing for the people who happened to be standing there.

Then over to American Whiskey for Meet Me at the Buzzer, shanelle julia rosita's closing reception for Homage to New York, her Knicks-inspired collection. The Visceral Glitch was there.

I finished at Broome Street Gallery Night at Cycol for Biz Markie and Bisco Smith, and that is where Cat Russell handed me a Magic: The Gathering deck box. It is one of the coolest things anybody has given me in this space, and he just gave it to me.

I closed the night at Candy's running our nightly WaveWarZ quick battles.

## What I am taking home

The room that did the least for me was the one with the most people in it.

The difference was never the venue, the city, or the guest list. It was whether one person in the room already knew me. Judasaca walking through a door turned a quiet floor into the best thing I did all weekend, and nothing about the floor changed.

That is the part a feed cannot do, and it is why we keep showing up in person.

Thank you to everyone who made room this weekend.
