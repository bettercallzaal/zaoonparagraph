year of the zabal day 268

8 days until ZAOstock, and I want to introduce the people who are actually going to play it.

Eight independent acts said yes to a free festival on a closed street in a town of this size. Some of them are driving a couple of hours. Three of them are travelling a very long way. Nobody is doing it for the money, because there is not very much of it. They are doing it because the bill is worth being on, and I am glad about every one of them.

Here they are, in the order they play.

**The Crown Vics** open at noon. They bill themselves as Maine's premier rock 'n' roll dance band, built on rockabilly, boogie woogie and honky tonk and twisted through classic rock and Americana. What sold me is how they describe their own sets: no long solos, no slow fades, hardly a breath between numbers. That is exactly what you want first on an open street, because a dance band with no gaps in the set is how a crowd gets built out of people who were only walking past.

**OPEN X** play second. A genre-blurring rock power pop trio with a cinematic sound and classical influences, and shows built for a stadium. Three people making a dense, symphonic wall of sound on a parklet in Ellsworth, early in the afternoon. I have been looking forward to that collision since we booked it.

**Grass Rug** are third, from Portland. Five of them, formed in 2025, playing indie jam rock out of psychedelic 60s and 70s rock, 90s alternative and 2010s indie. Their live sets are improvised rather than fixed, which means the version you hear on Franklin Street will not exist anywhere else.

**Acadia Rising** are fourth, and they are from Ellsworth itself. Original flute and hand percussion, blending global influences with the landscape here, built on expressive melodies, dynamic rhythms and improvisation. There is nothing else on this bill that sounds remotely like it, and putting it in the middle of the afternoon is deliberate.

**Michael Anderson** plays fifth, from Bar Harbor. A voice and a keyboard with roots in blues, soul and jazz, at home with standards and with popular hits. He says the thing I most want from someone playing an outdoor stage: "I find it very important to connect with my audience."

**DCoop** is sixth, coming from the DMV. Out of classic hip hop and then refusing to stay there, blending in reggae, rock, punk, tribal, country, EDM and R&B. He builds much of his music on spiritual frequencies, 432 to 900 Hz, meant to resonate rather than just play. That is his own description, and I would rather you hear it than have me explain it.

**LyonsDen** is seventh, travelling from the Onondaga Reservation. A Native American, self-produced independent artist working where reggae roots, hip-hop grit, blues soul, R&B melodies and EDM energy collide. Uplifting island vibes and raw Rez storytelling in the same song. Behind that there is an EP, an album, and a catalogue that runs from humorous punchline records to tracks about overcoming real adversity.

**Tom Fellenz** closes, coming from the SF Bay Area with one acoustic guitar. His originals are shaped by 70s and 80s progressive rock, smooth jazz and acoustic folk, and what people tell him is that his playing is cinematic and takes them somewhere. Eight acts in, after all of that noise, the day ends with one person and six strings. That shape is on purpose.

That is the bill. A rockabilly dance band, a stadium-sized trio, a jam band, flute and hand percussion, jazz piano and voice, genre-defying hip hop, Native reggae and hip-hop, and a solo acoustic guitarist, back to back from noon to 6.

[Every one of them has a page with their own words and their music.](https://zaostock.com/program)

ZAOstock is free, all ages, on Saturday, October 3, 2026, on the Franklin Street Parklet in downtown Ellsworth, Maine, from 12 to 6 pm Eastern. Rain or shine, under tent cover. At 6 the street clears and Black Moon Public House next door hosts its own evening.

There is no gate and no wristband. [RSVP for free](https://ticket.zaostock.com) so we know how much water, seating and shelter to plan for.
